Why Health Scoring Matters

Customer health scoring is one of the most important measures in the DripJobs Customer Success toolkit. It assesses each customer's engagement with the platform and their relationship with the organization — giving every team member a way to gauge whether a customer is satisfied, engaged, and likely to renew. Tracking it well surfaces upsell opportunities and, just as importantly, surfaces risk before it becomes a cancellation.

"The score is only as useful as the discipline behind it; a health score that is not updated is not a health score, it is a guess." CS Strategy, Structure & Playbooks · Section 9, pg. 12

Every customer with an active subscription carries a health score at all times, calculated through a combination of automation and personal interaction, and presented on a dashboard in the DripJobs company "King Kong" account (Super Admin view).

The Target Framework — Green / Yellow / Red

This is the long-term model documented in the Customer Team Guide, Section 9 (pg. 12–13). It's the framework we're training toward. It is not yet what HealthScore calculates automatically today — see Section 3 and the Correlation Guide below for how the two relate right now.

Health Status Labels

StatusDefinitionActions
🟢 Green · On Track Consistently engaged, logging in regularly, progressing through milestones, consistent platform activity. Low risk of churn. Maintain cadence with touchpoints, for new features and satisfaction reflected in surveys. Eligible for referral or testimonial at appropriate milestones.
🟡 Yellow · Needs Attention Signs of slowing engagement, features still to be activated, incomplete milestones, or reduced activity. Medium risk — intervention needed. Proactive personalized outreach, identify barriers, escalate if Yellow status persists beyond the defined threshold.
🔴 Red · High Risk Disengaged, missed multiple touchpoints, rare or no login, payments missing, no meaningful platform activity. High risk of cancellation. Immediate escalation to aligned departments, personalized connection to the customer. Save-conversation initiated, real reason for disengagement documented.

What's Live Today — HealthScore (V1)

What this guide calls HealthScore was previously referred to as the "Activation Dashboard." Same system, same build, new name — used consistently throughout this guide from here on.

HealthScore (Super Admin > King Kong) is the automated system that currently powers the Health column CS sees for every tenant account. It ships this round with the original six status labels, not the streamlined three-color model above.

The dashboard's Health column runs on just two hard data signals — Total Sales ≥ $10,000 and A2P (text messaging) activated — layered with account age and legacy status. It evaluates seven conditions in priority order (first match wins), which collapse into six visible badge states:

BadgeTrigger Condition
⚫ InactiveAccount is not active (cancelled/churned). Always wins regardless of other signals.

🟡 WarningNew account, fewer than 60 days remaining, and $10k or A2P milestone still unmet — or a legacy account with either milestone still unmet, regardless of age.

⚠️
Two Different "Health Scores" Coexist Right Now

The Health column within HealthScore is a narrow, automated, two-signal score (Total Sales + A2P). The CS Strategy Playbook's Green/Yellow/Red framework is a broader, human-judged relationship score across six categories (Onboarding, Platform Engagement, Support, Billing, Social, Advocacy). They're related in spirit and this guide maps them for training purposes, but they are not the same calculation. Don't tell a customer they're "Green" in one system and assume it automatically means Green in the other.

✅

Jeremy has confirmed the target is the 3-color system from Section 9 of the Playbook: Green / Yellow / Red. Inactive still sits outside the three-color model entirely (the "*" above); that part remains an open item, see Section 6.

5

Putting It Into Practice

Use the two systems for what each is actually good at, rather than treating them as interchangeable:

  • HealthScore (King Kong > Super Admin): the objective, automated signal. Best for spotting which accounts need attention right now, especially within the 90-day activation window for new accounts, or the $10k/A2P milestone check for legacy accounts.
  • CRM Health Status field (customer record): the human judgment call. Per Section 8 of the Playbook, update this — along with risk factors for engagement, notable behavior shifts, churn risk, and upsell opportunity — after every meaningful interaction, using the six-category Green/Yellow/Red framework from Section 2 above as the lens.

The Green/Yellow/Red language is already how reps talk about accounts in outreach planning today, even while the dashboard build catches up to it. For example, the Month 12 advocacy touchpoint in Section 6 of the Playbook reads:

"Celebration of one year with DripJobs, account in Green Health score: potential referral ask or testimonial request. If Yellow or Red Health Score: specific communication around the risk factors (usage, features)." CS Strategy, Structure & Playbooks · Section 6, pg. 8

Dashboard tells you who to look at. The CRM Health Status and six-category judgment tell you why, and what to say.
